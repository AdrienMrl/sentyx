This is a clip from a fisheye camera mounted ON the body of a parked car
(Tesla Sentry Mode). Parts of the car the camera is mounted on are visible at
the frame edges — usually a strip along the bottom, sometimes a dark strip up
one side. Because of the wide fisheye lens, neighboring parked cars routinely
look very large in the frame while being a normal parking gap away. Large in
frame does NOT mean close, and close does NOT mean touching.

Orientation, always true: the camera can NEVER see the car it is mounted on as
a complete car. The ego car appears ONLY as a cropped near-field strip at a
frame edge — often dark, blurred, or warped, and it is the CLOSEST surface in
the image; everything else is behind it. Never label any car visible in the
frame as the ego car: every complete vehicle you can see is a NEIGHBORING
vehicle, and a person opening its door, entering it, or sitting in it is
interacting with that neighbor, not with this car. Start your evidence by
stating where in the frame the ego-car strip is visible.

Decide whether anything physically TOUCHED this car. The touch point itself is
almost never visible — the camera sits on the car, so contact happens at the
frame edge or below it, often while the object blocks the view. You must infer
contact from motion, and only from these specific observable events:

1. STOPPED ARC: a door from an adjacent vehicle swings open toward this car
   and its arc STOPS and rests while the occupant continues getting in or out
   — the door stopped because it met this car. A door that swings open, stops
   short, and swings back without pausing did not touch. A door HELD wide
   open toward the camera for many seconds, its edge reaching the frame edge
   where the ego-car strip is, while its owner climbs in or out, is RESTING
   on this car: contact. For any adjacent-car door opened toward the camera,
   track its outer bottom corner frame by frame: if it advances and then
   holds still — or slides behind the ego-car strip — while the person keeps
   moving, the door is resting on this car. And like rule 2's clip-start
   case: a door ALREADY at full arc at t=0 — the recording was triggered by
   that door meeting this car — but ONLY when ALL THREE hold: (i) the door
   stays at full arc for many seconds, (ii) a person physically climbs
   through the doorway during that hold (the door is being used as a passage
   while resting), and (iii) the door's outer corner reaches the frame edge
   and visually overlaps or occludes the ego-car strip. If any of the three
   is missing — the door soon closes, its occupant is already seated, its
   corner sits in mid-frame with ground or background visible around it —
   it is a neighbor using its own door inside a normal gap: NOT contact. A
   neighbor that opens or closes its own doors and drives off without
   frame-fill, jolt, or ego-strip occlusion never touched this car.
2. EXTREME NEAR-FIELD: an object ARRIVES during the clip and grows until it
   fills the frame to the bottom edge and corners, its shape warped by the
   fisheye — far past where any neighboring car normally sits. A vehicle
   whose rear or side fills essentially the whole frame is pressing against
   this car. The reverse also proves it: Sentry clips are triggered BY the
   event, so a clip often begins at the moment of contact — a vehicle that at
   t=0 ALREADY fills the frame edge to edge (a license plate or body panel
   huge and centered, far past any normal gap) and then pulls away was
   resting against this car when the clip began. A neighbor whose full side
   profile is visible with ground under it is just parked; a panel filling
   everything with no surroundings visible is pressing.
3. MOTION ENDS ON THE CAR: a person's movement terminates at this car and
   they stay — leaning over it, resting against it, sitting on it, placing a
   hand or foot that bears weight. Their body overlaps the visible ego-car
   strip at the frame edge while their walking motion has stopped. Judge the
   person's BODY, not their intent: someone whose business is with a
   NEIGHBORING car (loading a child, grabbing a bag) may still stop walking
   and lean, stretch, or steady themselves over THIS car while they do it —
   their torso looming over the camera, filling the bottom of the frame,
   feet planted at the ego strip. That is contact with this car even though
   their attention is on the neighbor.
4. JOLT: the image itself jolts, shakes, or shifts at the moment an object
   arrives at or leaves the car.
5. OCCLUSION: the ego-car strip is the closest surface in the image, so a
   moving object (a door edge, a foot, a hand, a bumper) whose leading edge
   visually slides BEHIND or UNDER the ego-car strip has physically crossed
   onto this car's body: contact. Static neighbors partially hidden by the
   strip all along do not count — only an edge that moves into occlusion
   during the clip.
6. AFTERMATH: people betray contact after it happens. Someone who pauses to
   inspect this car's panel, bends or leans to look at the spot where their
   door or cart met it, rubs or wipes it, or looks it over with visible
   concern before leaving — that behavior is evidence the touch was real,
   even when the touch itself was not resolvable. A person who walks off
   without a glance suggests nothing happened.

Everything else is NOT contact. In particular:
- A person walking or squeezing past at constant, uninterrupted speed is not
  contact, no matter how close, even if their hand or bag swings near the
  panel, and even if something they carry briefly blocks the whole camera
  view while they pass. Blocking the LENS's view is not touching the CAR: if
  the walking never pauses, nothing touched.
- A neighboring parked car that is simply large in frame — static the whole
  clip, or arriving/leaving with an ordinary parking gap, no frame-fill, no
  jolt — is not contact.
- People opening doors of, entering, or leaving a NEIGHBORING car are not
  contact unless event 1 happened to a door aimed at this car.
- "Probably brushed it" is not an observation. If you cannot name the second
  and which of the four events you saw, the answer is contact=false.

In "evidence", state the second and which numbered event you observed, or the
strongest near-miss and which requirement it failed.

Report contact_probability: your honest probability in [0,1] that something
physically touched this car, combining all six kinds of evidence. Calibrate:
a clear rule match, or a partial match corroborated by aftermath behavior,
is 0.85+; a partial pattern alone — a door held at full arc over a tight gap
where the touch itself cannot be resolved, an object at extreme close range
without a visible stop — belongs in 0.4-0.7, not at 0; a close pass with
smooth uninterrupted motion is 0.1 or less; an empty or clearly clear scene
is near 0. Do not collapse genuine uncertainty to 0 or 1.

threat: "none" if nothing touched the car and nothing threatened it. If
contact happened, set threat by WHAT touched: any VEHICLE part touching this
car is "high" — a door that met this car (a swung door strikes edge-first:
dings and dents), a bumper or body panel pressing against it, any collision —
and so is deliberate interference. "low" is only for a light HUMAN touch with
no damage risk: a lean, a hand, a foot, brushing past.
